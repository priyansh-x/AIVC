# Phase 1 — Problem space inside associate-stack collapse, India

## TL;DR
- The Indian VC associate workflow is real, repetitive, and mostly *un-productised* — funds buy 90+ point-tools (Affinity, Tracxn, Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity) but no India-grounded workflow product wraps them; Taghash + MCP is the closest, and even Blume runs on Taghash at the firm level while its partners still operate from Apple Notes + Gmail ([workflow-map](workflow-map.md)).
- Three sub-wedges score real: **(A) cold-pitch triage** (fastest dogfoodable), **(B) India-grounded comp pull** (highest dataset-moat fit), **(C) templated memo drafting** (highest demo appeal, most crowded). The 3000+ Indian-startup dataset is *most distinctive* in (B), *useful* in (A), *generic-grounding* in (C) ([wedge-candidates](wedge-candidates.md)).
- **CEO decision:** lead Phase 2 customer discovery with **sub-wedge A (triage) as the operational lead and sub-wedge B (comp pull) as the defensibility hook**. Defer sub-wedge C — it is the seductive distraction (memo drafting is the easiest to demo, hardest to defend, and Claude already half-owns it in Indian funds).

## Key findings (max 5)
1. **Indian seed/A funds process ~3,500 pitches/yr each; cold pitches get 1–5 minutes of analyst time** ([Sajith Pai Blume, one-year reflections](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-sajith-pai-reflections-one-year.md)). 100X.VC's published cohort funnel — 4,785 → 458 → 10 funded — confirms the same shape at sharper extremes ([[100x-vc]]). Triage is the single most compressible activity in the workflow.
2. **Indian-built VC tooling is data-layer-deep but workflow-layer-shallow.** Taghash (60+ fund customers incl. Blume, Kalaari, A91), Tracxn (public, 678K Indian startups), Sanchi Connect (deeptech sourcing), Venture Intelligence — all exist; none own the *associate's daily decision flow* ([Taghash](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-taghash-india-vc-os.md), [Tracxn](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-tracxn-india.md), [stack catalogue](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-indian-vc-tech-stack.md)).
3. **AI is already a layer inside Indian VC stacks (Claude for memos, Perplexity for research, ChatGPT for playbook GPTs) — but there is no India-grounded AI-for-VC *product*.** Penetration is ad hoc and unmeasured; the Taghash 2025 survey of 50+ Indian funds measures investment-thesis AI focus (74% AI-first), not internal-workflow AI adoption ([Taghash survey](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-taghash-indian-vc-survey.md), [stack catalogue](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-indian-vc-tech-stack.md)).
4. **The Indian comp-pull problem is structurally worse than the US version** — iSAFE-blurred post-money, cross-border Delaware/Singapore flips, undisclosed ARR, contradicting press numbers. MCA/ROC filings are richer than US public records for private cos but unstructured. This is exactly where the 3000+ dataset compounds, if it includes funding history ([workflow-map §4](workflow-map.md), [MCA source](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-mca-roc-india-diligence.md)).
5. **Indian VC deal volume rose 880 → 1,270 in 2024 (+45%); 95% of deals are <$50M; fundraising fell 35% but maiden funds = 1/3 of capital raised** ([Bain India VC 2025](../../brain/sources/2026-05-31-bain-india-vc-report-2025.md)). Translation: deal-doing work is growing, capital is bottlenecked, *new* funds (AIVC-shaped) are still being formed.

## So what
The Indian VC associate workflow is a real, daily, painful job — and it is structurally underserved because Indian-built tooling stopped at data (Tracxn) and operating-system (Taghash) and never reached the associate-decision layer. AIVC's wedge inside this gap is credible: sub-wedge A is dogfoodable on day one against AIVC's own incoming pipeline, sub-wedge B is where the proprietary dataset is most distinctively a moat. The decision implied: **Phase 2 customer-discovery interviews should target the analyst/associate seat at small-to-mid Indian seed funds (10–25 person firms like Blume, Stellaris, 3one4, Pi, Together; and 1–3 person boutiques like 100X.VC), with sub-wedge A as the conversation lead and sub-wedge B as the moat hook.** Defer sub-wedge C.

## Open questions
- What is the *actual schema* of the 3000+ Indian-startup dataset? Sub-wedge B's defensibility is binary on whether funding-round history is present.
- How many Indian seed funds skip the formal IC memo for iSAFE rounds? If "most," sub-wedge C TAM shrinks and the decision to defer it firms up.
- What's measured AI-tool penetration at Indian funds today — Bessemer-equivalent benchmarks? No Indian published number exists; Phase 2 interviews must produce one.
