## What we learned

### 14:00 — From the Phase 0 work
- **Of 12 atomic VC activities, only 2 are genuinely transformative for AI in 2026:** screening + memo drafting (the associate-stack collapse) and portfolio support (the a16z platform-team collapse). 4 are meaningful, 6 are cosmetic or unmoved. The wedge has to live in one of the two transformative buckets, or be a brand-led play on contrarian taste augmented by AI. ([artifact](../../problem/operating-model-decomposition.md))
- **"AI is the investor" is marketing, not operations, in 2026.** Even Agent Fund (the most AI-native fund in market) keeps humans on commit. LLM hallucination rates (8–52% across 2026 benchmarks) plus fiduciary accountability foreclose Position 5 this decade. ([artifact](../../problem/ai-native-vc-first-principles.md))
- **Correlation Ventures is the cautionary tale.** Pure-quant VC since 2010 → ~$500M AUM, co-invest only. Tooling without sourcing access or brand caps you at modest AUM. AI alone is not a fundraising story.
- **Surprise:** the top platform VCs (a16z, Accel, Floodgate) *buy* Harmonic rather than build their own sourcing data layer. The sourcing-data wedge is already commodifying — "better data" is dead as a moat for a new firm.
- **Counter-surprise:** SignalFire just raised $1B (April 2025) explicitly on the proprietary-AI narrative. The reconciliation: their moat isn't the data, it's 12 years of labeled outcomes *against* the data. A new firm can't replicate that.

## What we learned

- **India's VC tech stack is data-deep, workflow-shallow.** Roughly 90+ tools are catalogued for Indian fund use, but only four are India-built (Taghash, Tracxn, Venture Intelligence, Sanchi Connect) and *none* own the associate's daily decision flow. Taghash is the closest — it ships a VC operating system and was first to market with a VC-specific MCP server bridging fund data into Claude/OpenAI agents — but it's plumbing, not an AI-for-VC product. The workflow layer between "raw data" and "associate decision" is the open seat.
- **Concrete Indian funnel data**, never before in the brain: Blume processes ~3,500 pitches/yr with 1/6–1/5 referrals; cold pitches get 1–5 min each. 100X.VC's published cohort: 4,785 → 458 shortlisted → 10 funded (1% close). Sajith Pai works 60+ hours/week and has 60–70 emails pile up after meeting days. ([workflow-map](../../problem/workflow-map.md))
- **The dataset moat is sharpest at comp pull, not screening.** Indian valuation comps are uniquely noisy (iSAFE post-money blur, Delaware/Singapore flips, undisclosed ARR, contradicting press numbers). MCA/ROC filings have richer cap-table data than US public records but are unstructured PDFs nobody has ingested. *This* is where the 3000+ dataset is most distinctively Indian and most distinctively ours — if it includes funding-round history.
- **Hype-vs-substance check:** the Taghash 2025 Indian VC Survey (50+ firms) measures investment-thesis AI focus at 74% — but is silent on *internal* AI tooling adoption. Indian funds market themselves as "AI investors" much louder than they market themselves as "AI-operated." Real operating-AI penetration is anecdotal only; no Indian Bessemer-equivalent benchmark exists. Phase 2 interviews must produce one.
- **Surprise:** Blume is a public Taghash customer at the firm level, but Sajith Pai's published personal stack is Vimcal + Apple Notes + Gmail + Alfred — no CRM. The CRM-nobody-uses pattern (Evertrace's headline failure mode globally) is alive at India's best-known seed fund.
- **Counter-surprise:** Tracxn coverage is enormous (678K Indian startups in DB; 34.1K funded), but reviews flag pricing as "high in India context, no monthly option" — which prices out exactly the smaller/newer Indian funds that most need a workflow layer on top. There's room below Tracxn.
